Why Girls with Goals

The window is adolescence. The time is now.

The Problem

A girl can be talented, hardworking and top of her class, and still leave school with no idea what to do next. Not because she lacks ambition. Because nobody has shown her the road from her timetable to a career.

In many school systems, career guidance is a single subject, if it exists at all. A girl who cannot see how her subjects connect to a future is more likely to lose interest, fall behind and leave school. When that happens, doors close early. Girls drop out. Girls fall pregnant. Girls end up in relationships they cannot leave because they have no income of their own.

The numbers say the same thing. Youth unemployment is rising in many regions.

South Africa

In South Africa, where we are based,

51%

of people aged 15 to 29 are unemployed.

Education does not automatically become economic participation.

Why Adolescence

Adolescence is when a girl decides who she is, what she believes she can do, and which subjects she will keep or drop.

Support that arrives in adulthood arrives after those decisions have been made, often by someone else.

The window to act is now, while she is still choosing.

1

2

Why It Is Free to the Girl

The girls who need this journal most cannot afford it.

If girls or their families had to pay, the journal would reach the girls who need it least.

So the model is turned around. An institution or organisation buys in bulk. The girl receives her copy free. Her access depends on her need, never on what her family can spend.

What It Is

Girls with Goals: A Career Colouring and Confidence Journal for Girls is a practical, self guided life skills and goal setting system for girls in high school.

It is robust enough to deliver consistent outcomes and flexible enough to be adapted for every country.

What It Does for the Girl

1

Identity and confidence.

She learns who she is and what she is capable of.

2

Values based decision making.

She learns to choose with clarity, not pressure.

3

Education and career goal setting.

She connects her subjects to real careers and sets her own goals.

4

Planning and resilience.

She learns to plan, track her progress and keep going.

What Is Inside

Careers explained plainly.

Each career begins with what the person actually does, and which school subjects a girl needs to get there.

Colouring pages that build a connection.

A girl colours in the engineer, the doctor or the lawyer herself, so she takes part in creating the woman she could become.

Representation on every page.

Different skin tones, hair, body shapes, faiths and heritage, so no girl turns the page and finds herself missing.

Challenges and reflection.

Easy to follow activities that build confidence and help her set her own goals.

Made for Every Country

School subjects are named differently around the world.

Each edition of Girls with Goals carries its own country name and its own subject terms.

The South African edition, for example, uses South African subject names such as Life Sciences.

Career Expo 2026

Career Expo 2026: Report

Tuesday, 20 October 2026 | Expo Centre Johannesburg, Hall 1

The Day at a Glance

  • More than 500 girls in Grade 10 and 11
  • More than 100 girls from Rustenburg, travelling in by bus
  • Girls from underserved no fee schools in Johannesburg and Rustenburg
  • Careers in mining, medicine and law, presented by people who work in them
  • Colleges and employers on the expo floor
  • Speakers close to the girls’ own age
  • Security and an ambulance on site, and event liability insurance in place
  • No cost to any girl or her family

Summary

On Tuesday, 20 October 2026, Ascend Girls Network held its first Career Expo.

More than 500 girls from underserved schools in Johannesburg and Rustenburg spent the day meeting people who work in careers they had never seen up close.

Sponsors paid for the day and we arranged the buses, so no girl paid anything to attend.

For many of them, it was the first time they sat across from a person doing the career they had only heard about.

Why We Held the Expo

The girls who came are in Grade 10 and 11, at schools that serve families who have very little.

They go to school every day, but many cannot say why they are studying mathematics or physical science.

Life Orientation is the only formal career guidance most of them receive.

A girl who cannot see where her subjects lead is more likely to drop out.

The Expo exists to show her where they can take her.

What the Girls Experienced

Careers Up Close

The girls met people working in mining, medicine and law, asked how they got there, and heard which subjects opened the door.

Speakers Close to Their Own Age

The founders of Tol Thema and Umkho AI, both born after the year 2000, told the girls how they built something real.

Colleges on the Expo Floor

Colleges explained what to study and how to apply.

Professionals at Their Stands

Partners brought their own people to speak with the girls.

At the Lusenga Inc stand, the girls met attorneys, conveyancers and notaries and asked about the work.

Competitions and Prizes

The girls took part in competitions, and many went home with prizes.

In Their Words

“A girl told us she never knew that mining careers were also for women until she saw them.”
“Another girl told us she was inspired when she listened to the founder and CEO of Umkho AI, who she said is only 22 years old.”

The first girl learnt that a career was open to her. The second saw someone her own age doing something she could aim for.

Who Was There

Girls and teachers from schools across Johannesburg and Rustenburg.
